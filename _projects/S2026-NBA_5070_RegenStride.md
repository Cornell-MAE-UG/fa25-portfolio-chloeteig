## **Overview**

I completed this project as part of NBA 5070 (Entrepreneurship for Scientists & Engineers) at Cornell. Working on a six-person team, we developed RegenStride, a B2B licensing venture built around a NASA-patented self-healing polymer technology. The polymer, PBG-BCB-PBG, restores structural integrity in under 100 microseconds when mechanical force is applied, making it uniquely suited for athletic footwear midsoles that degrade after approximately 400 miles of use.

Rather than pursuing a direct-to-consumer path, we designed a licensing-first business model targeting a single elite OEM partner as our entry point into the $131B global athletic footwear market.

---

## **The Technology**

The core IP is US Patent 10,745,509 B2, developed by NASA researchers Gordon and Siochi. The polymer uses mechanophore-linked chemistry: when damage applies force to the polymer backbone, benzocyclobutene groups undergo electrocyclic ring opening and covalent bonds re-form, restoring cushioning and energy return without any external intervention.

No competing product on the market offers retained cushioning as a core value proposition. We validated this through over 50 customer discovery interviews with runners, footwear professionals, and OEM engineers.

---

## **Business Model**

We structured RegenStride as a licensing business, earning 8-10% royalties per OEM unit sold rather than manufacturing product ourselves. Our first target customer was not the runner, but the materials and product development engineering team at Nike, chosen for their deep R&D infrastructure, established limited edition playbook, and global reach to amplify Phase 2 mass market adoption.

## **Go-to-Market Strategy**

Our GTM strategy phased commercialization across three stages: a limited-edition premium validation drop with Nike, integration into core product lines at scale, and eventual multi-OEM expansion. We projected Phase 1 revenue of $80-100K from 1,000 premium units, scaling to $1M-$7M annually once integrated into core SKUs at 100K-1M units per year.

## **Financial Projections**

We modeled unit economics around a $5.60-$7.00 per unit licensing fee at an 8-10% royalty rate, with LTV:CAC of 1:7 to 1:10. Our funding roadmap outlined a pre-seed raise of $750K-$1M for polymer optimization and OEM pilot, followed by a $2M-$5M seed round for commercial integration and manufacturing scale-up.
